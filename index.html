Damla Görgülü.

Department of Engineering, University of Cambridge

I’m an MPhil student in Electrical and Electronic Engineering at the University of Cambridge, supported by the Turkish Education Foundation (UK) Cambridge Masters Scholarship in collaboration with the Cambridge Trust. I’m also a member of Robinson College. I completed a double major in Electrical & Electronics Engineering and Computer Engineering at Koç University, graduating summa cum laude. Along the way, I’ve worked on RISC-V processors, FPGA systems and emerging spintronic logic, and completed three internships at the Technical University of Munich.

My interests lie in digital design, VLSI and integrated circuits, particularly hardware for AI. I’d like to help build the next generation of microprocessors and AI accelerators, exploring how processor design, memory and the underlying devices can work together to make computing more efficient and reliable.

Connecting memory and AI accelerators

2025 – 2026

Technical University of Munich · Chair of AI Processor Design

I started by exploring DRAM behaviour and bringing up RISC-V systems on OrangeCrab and ButterStick FPGAs. The aim was to use their onboard memory to support a Cheshire RISC-V SoC. This grew into work on S-Link communication between the chips, organising how neural-network weights and biases are stored and transferred.

Exploring alternatives to CMOS

2025 – 2026

Koç University · Assoc. Prof. Mehmet Cengiz Onbaşlı

With my teammates, I compared conventional CMOS circuits with skyrmion-based logic, an emerging approach that uses magnetic structures to compute. We built a workflow to compare a few computing blocks and explore the trade-offs in size, speed and energy use.

How circuits age

Summer 2024

Technical University of Munich · Prof. Hussam Amrouch

I studied how transistor ageing affects the speed of AI processor circuits. I worked with CARAT, a custom ageing-simulation framework developed by the AI Processor Design lab, adapting it for 3 nm and 14 nm technologies and testing arithmetic circuits.

Building a RISC-V processor

2023 – 2024

Koç University · TEKNOFEST 2024 finalist

Together with two peers, I built a RISC-V-based processor that runs a small neural network to recognise handwritten digits. We developed the hardware in VHDL and Verilog and put together a Python interface to send inputs and test the design.
